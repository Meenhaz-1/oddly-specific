# Editorial decision calibration

Use these cases to calibrate product taste, not as factual evidence. Mandatory source verification overrides every example below.

## ACCEPT patterns

### Pen-cap vent hole

Accept a well-worded version even though a player might initially guess a ring-shaped sweet or another perforated object. Plausible wrong guesses are part of solving. They become an ambiguity failure only when they satisfy the exact wording as completely as the intended answer.

### Norwegian Wood and the sitar

Do not reject automatically because an experienced player may recall the recording. Excluding Ravi Shankar provides useful elimination, and identifying both song and instrument can still produce an enjoyable reveal. Partial recall is compatible with a strong score when the construction and underlying fact remain rewarding.

### Kedgeree from khichdi

Do not reject merely because the final step may require some culinary knowledge. The transformation from a rice-and-lentil dish into a British breakfast dish, preserved through the name, supplies an interesting linguistic and historical payoff.

### Hundi rather than hawala

An adjacent answer from the same conceptual family is not automatically a second fully valid answer. Assess the historical period, requested category, and exact function. If the wording clearly asks for a period-specific financial instrument and one answer is more precise, prefer a wording improvement over reflexive rejection.

## REWRITE pattern

### Shipping-container standardization

The underlying fact is strong, but rewrite when the stem explains that the real innovation was interoperability before asking the question. Remove the explanation of the mechanism and ask what object or feature had to be standardized. Preserve the global transport clues; reserve dimensions and corner fittings for the reveal.

## REJECT patterns

### The at-sign in email

Reject when the stem mentions email, displays the symbol, translates it as "at the rate of," and then asks for the symbol. These clues do not create a route; together they state the answer repeatedly. A rewritten question may use the underlying history only if it can remove the literal and domain giveaways.

### Bluetooth and Harald Bluetooth

Reject the familiar version that describes a Scandinavian king uniting tribes and asks for the wireless technology. It is a common quiz chestnut and the metaphor makes the answer too familiar. A fresh, independently solvable construction would need genuinely new evidence, not merely different wording.

## Calibration principles

- Do not reject a question merely because it has one decisive clue, includes an element of recall, or attracts plausible wrong guesses.
- Distinguish a useful clue from leakage: useful clues require a reasoning step; leakage states, translates, depicts, or defines the answer.
- Reward a strong premise and satisfying explanation even when the inference path is not elaborate.
- Treat an alternative as disqualifying only when it satisfies the exact stem at least as well as the intended answer.
- Be strict about common chestnuts, over-explanation, flat reveals, and unsupported facts.
